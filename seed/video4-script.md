### Moth holes in cashmere: fixing ten tiny holes

Ten tiny moth holes in a cashmere jumper looks like a disaster. It is not. Pinprick moth holes are the easiest repair in knitwear — if you treat each one on its own, and if you stop the moths first. This video covers both.

#### 1. Stop the damage before you mend anything

Moth holes keep appearing if the larvae are still in the fibre. Before you pick up a needle, seal the jumper in a bag and put it in the freezer for forty-eight hours. That kills anything living in it. Then let it come back to room temperature inside the bag so condensation stays off the wool. Mending first and freezing later means mending twice.

#### 2. Pinprick holes: yes, each one separately

The question is always whether to fix ten small holes as one big repair. No — each pinprick hole gets its own tiny repair, and each takes about a minute. A pinprick hole in a knit is one or two broken loops, and the danger is not the hole, it is the ladder: every broken loop wants to run. Working each hole separately keeps every repair invisible and stops you cutting healthy fabric into one big weak patch.

Work over a darning mushroom, an egg, or an orange — anything smooth and curved that opens the knit without stretching it. Use a fine yarn of the same weight as the jumper, split down to a single ply for cashmere, and a blunt tapestry needle so you slide between fibres instead of splitting them.

For each hole: find the loose loops at the top and bottom of the hole, catch them on the needle, and rebuild the missing stitch by tracing the path of the knitting itself — over, under, and back through the loop above. This is duplicate stitch, and on a pinprick hole it means two or three stitches at most. Anchor the tail ends on the inside with a small stitch each; never knot cashmere, a knot wears a new hole from the inside.

#### 3. When a hole is bigger than a pinprick

If one of the ten is the size of a pea or larger, the duplicate-stitch trick is not enough — that one needs a small woven darn, worked loosely. Cashmere stretches more than ordinary wool, so keep the tension looser than feels right; a tight darn on a soft knit puckers into a dimple that shows more than the hole did.

#### 4. Afterwards

Check the spots moths love and people miss: under the arms, along the side seams, and wherever the jumper was folded in the drawer. Ten pinprick holes is about ten minutes of mending once the first one has taught your hands the motion. Store the jumper clean — moths eat the meal you left on the fibre, not the fibre itself.
